# Assessment methodology

## Evidence and discovery

Inspect existing context first. Track coverage as sufficient, partial or unknown for background, responsibilities, competencies, outcomes, ownership/authority, preferences, aspirations and constraints. Coverage is not ability. Ask one question at a time and permit provisional output. Distinguish user-reported facts from independently verified facts, analytical inferences and unknowns. Cite external sources with dates and relevance. Never invent evidence.

Verify public claims before use: role definitions, job descriptions, market conditions, compensation and learning resources. Cross-reference important or conflicting claims. If browsing is unavailable, mark market claims unverified and omit unsupported conclusions; ask the user for personal facts rather than searching for private details.

## Adaptive competency dimensions

Choose dimensions relevant to the profession and evidence; explain their selection. Do not force a technology rubric onto other industries. Proposed common ordinal scale (exact anchors require validation):

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Evidence of not yet demonstrating the competency |
| 1 | Awareness |
| 2 | Practitioner |
| 3 | Independent application |
| 4 | Advanced design, complex problem-solving or guidance |
| 5 | Expert/authority; demonstrated direction or broad impact |
| Unknown | Insufficient evidence; never substitute zero |

Record evidence references, rationale, gaps and confidence for each assessment. Distinguish demonstrated expertise from formal authority; do not infer either from title alone. Confidence reflects evidence quality, not probability.

## Separate outputs

Professional DNA describes demonstrated competencies. Role similarity describes alignment of actual responsibilities to verified role definitions. Readiness compares evidence against target requirements and gaps. Preference reflects user-stated interests and constraints. Do not collapse these into one score. No statistical probabilities, false precision or unsupported claims of psychometric validity. Score aggregation, calibration and numeric conversion are open decisions; qualitative results are acceptable when evidence is limited.

## Career positioning and growth

Choose and explain relevant axes, e.g. technical depth and organizational scope for technology roles. Label plots as conceptual unless a real dataset and method justify statistical clustering. Preserve headroom, direct labels and uncertainty; distinguish current role from aspiration. Compare multiple paths including specialist and management alternatives where relevant. Recommend learning tied to gaps, capacity, work opportunities and measurable outcomes. Verify learning resources. Consider mandate, decision rights and accountability separately.

## Reflection and safety

Challenge assumptions respectfully; do not flatter, discriminate, diagnose or promise career outcomes. Do not use protected or irrelevant personal traits to score ability. User confirms or corrects conclusions before baseline persistence. A change in dimensions or rubric makes historical scores non-comparable unless explicitly reconciled.
